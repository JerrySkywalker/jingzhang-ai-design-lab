## 1. Premises tried

Seven internal hypotheses were tested without candidate numbering:

1. **Thick institutional thresholds** — public-to-shared-to-controlled depth between the heritage/public field and secure campuses or enterprises.
2. **Enterprise space ladder** — adaptable rooms, workshop/courtyard blocks and metropolitan mixed-use buildings supporting different firm stages.
3. **Compute-to-city municipal exchange** — conditional interfaces between verified AI loads and energy, heat, water, cooling, maintenance and public receivers.
4. **Time-sharing city** — building and public-space design around day/evening/night handover before new construction.
5. **The serviced city** — logistics, waste, repair, shift change, worker welfare and event recovery as a prior urban layer.
6. **Fine-grain renewal field** — retain/open/subdivide/infill/replace actions derived from unequal fabrics rather than one corridor module.
7. **Level-access reliability** — redundant step-free routes and refuges whose weakest transition controls admission.

The full one-sentence problem, judgment, geometry, three-area difference, AI role, nearest competitors and kill reason for each premise are in `PREMISE_TOURNAMENT.md`.

## 2. Why no C04 was selected

All seven failed at least one hard pre-admission test:

- H1, H2, H4, H5 and H6 can draw a city and cover most complete-design systems, but their transferable core fails P2 and their strongest forms collide with mature current proposals.
- H3 makes AI materially affect municipal design, but it is already a crowded compute/heat territory and depends on site utility facts that do not exist in the current official package. Making it the city would cross K6 and risk K8.
- H7 has a credible public-space dependency and can survive site substitution better, but it is a mobility/access subsystem. It cannot generate the innovation ecosystem and complete city without add-ons, so P3/K7 kills it.

This is the decisive conclusion: a good subsystem is not automatically a new mother premise, and a plausible story is not a site fact.
