# Neighbourhood Completeness Contract

```text
SYNTHETIC FRAMEWORK
NOT A CLAIM THAT ANY JING-ZHANG AREA PASSES
```

## Contract

An innovation neighbourhood is independently complete only when every required role can execute its ordinary-day service chains through identifiable physical places, at the relevant time, without compulsory digital access, while retaining a bounded fallback when one facility or the AI layer is unavailable.

Passing requires all of the following:

1. **Role coverage:** researcher, student, resident/family, older adult, disabled user, service worker, visitor and maintenance worker are tested separately. An average score cannot hide one excluded role.
2. **Chain coverage:** arrival, work/study/home, affordable food, toilet, basic care where relevant, non-commercial refuge, human help and worker/service space form connected physical chains rather than a facility checklist.
3. **Time coverage:** weekday, evening and weekend are separate states. A weekday campus does not pass as a neighbourhood.
4. **Weather coverage:** rain/heat tests require a protected route, wait or refuge for exposure-sensitive steps. Digital warnings cannot replace shelter.
5. **Event coexistence:** an event cannot evict toilets, refuge, access, affordable food or service labour from the ordinary day.
6. **Non-digital equality:** when digital service is unavailable or refused, the chain still completes through fixed information, cash/ordinary payment where relevant and people.
7. **Single-facility resilience:** closing one civic facility does not collapse several unrelated chains. Redundancy may be distributed, but it must be physically and temporally evidenced.
8. **Local/external distinction:** a service outside the unit or reachable only through a federation is recorded as an external dependency, not counted as local completeness. Specialist regional services may remain external, but ordinary daily chains cannot all be exported.
9. **Maintenance reality:** staff access, changing/rest, storage, toilets, waste and repair belong to completeness; they are not invisible back-office assumptions.
10. **Evidence gate:** a real unit passes only with official/cleared geometry, facilities and hours, accessible travel-time/route evidence, observation and responsible operators. Proximity on a map is insufficient.

The computational gate applies a strict conjunction: all eight personas must complete all required local chains in all seven states. This deliberately prevents a glossy aggregate score from masking structural exclusion. Professional design may refine the exact chains; it may not erase failed roles or states through weighting.

## What the synthetic experiment establishes

- A redundant mixed unit can pass the formal logic because it includes local, all-time, non-digital alternatives.
- A workday innovation enclave fails evening, weekend, weather, event and digital-outage tests despite containing research space.
- A federation-dependent unit fails local completeness when its everyday services are assigned to a remote shared hub.

These are logic tests only. No Jing-Zhang facility, user, opening time or catchment was modelled.

## ORDINARY_DAY_COMPLETENESS_GATE

For any candidate area, publish:

| Field | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Unit hypothesis | proposed boundary and why it is one daily unit |
| Personas | eight required roles plus locally evidenced additions |
| Chains | needs → places → route/time → non-digital path → fallback |
| States | weekday, evening, weekend, rain/heat, event, one facility down, digital down |
| External dependencies | service, reason external, travel/operating evidence and failure fallback |
| Failed roles/states | explicit; never averaged away |
| Spatial change | land use, ground floor, public realm, mobility, service/labour and phasing change required to pass |
| Evidence status | fact, derived, assumption or concept |

If the unit boundary or facility/hours evidence is missing, the result is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, not `PASS`.
