# Applying the Ordinary-Day Completeness Gate to Candidate 01

Candidate 01 currently fails to demonstrate neighbourhood completeness. Its strong documentation of automated states, maintenance and fallback does not provide verified daily service chains for eight roles.

The gate forces these changes:

- add housing, affordable food, childcare/basic care, toilets, refuge, human help and worker-support evidence rather than assuming mixed use equals availability;
- test evening/weekend use and service/maintenance shifts, not only demonstrations and pilot hours;
- provide fixed, staffed and ordinary operational paths when AI or the shared infrastructure is unavailable;
- keep event and robotic service edges from displacing ordinary walking, rest, toilets and affordable services;
- show how each key area serves existing workers and users, not only researchers, developers, visitors and operators;
- treat a service elsewhere along the spine as an external dependency, not proof that the local area is complete;
- add one-facility-down and bad-weather sections to the three key-area designs.

The gate does not require C01 to become Candidate 02 or to claim three complete neighbourhoods. It requires C01's technical spine to sit inside a demonstrably usable ordinary city.
