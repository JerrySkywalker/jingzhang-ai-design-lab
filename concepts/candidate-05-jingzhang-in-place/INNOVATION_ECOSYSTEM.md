# Innovation Ecosystem

## Proposition

Candidate 05 treats innovation as a relationship among existing carriers, public-side interfaces, eligible controlled rooms, professional/customer services and ordinary urban life. It does not map a firm lifecycle from north to south and does not invent partnerships, shared compute or migration flows.

## 43.6 km² roles

| Resource/actor class | Spatial interface | Candidate contribution | Unknown/consent |
|---|---|---|---|
| universities/research | controlled gate + public-side learning/project rooms | scheduled exchange without opening the campus as street | access, partnership, rooms, IP/data rules |
| early teams/independent builders | small adaptable ordinary/project rooms | reversible public/controlled carriers near daily life | demand, affordability, lease/operator |
| validation/testing organisations | eligible controlled carrier and service/safety edge | bounded model/equipment validation in verified fabric | carrier, safety, fire/power/data, operator |
| enterprises | verified mixed work/customer premises | adoption/use interface without a sequential growth belt | firm demand/movement and title |
| professional/capital services | staffed public/controlled rooms | compliance, IP, finance/customer access as services, not promised institutions | service partners and governance |
| residents/public bodies | ordinary city problems and public benefit | consultation, public-side services and opt-in bounded demonstrations | data/consent/authority |
| compute/data providers | service agreement/interface | minimum sufficient resource for a justified task | capacity, location, availability and security |
| talent/ordinary services | housing/community, food, care, learning, mobility | continuity and public-interest foundation | quantity, eligibility and funding |

## Three-area ecosystem expression

- north weights controlled validation and equipment/service interfaces, but also public learning and worker/visitor life;
- middle weights small project/learning interfaces at public-side campus/park/transit thresholds, with community/talent uses;
- south weights adoption, professional/customer service and urban-use learning in verified mixed carriers.

All areas contain research, enterprise, public and ordinary components in different proportions. No area is only one lifecycle stage.

## AI planning innovation

AI matters through spatial requirements and operating architecture, not branding:

- controlled model/equipment validation requires safe state, supervision, service/reset and reusable fit-out;
- secure project/data work requires public/controlled separation, minimum resources, human review and ordinary reuse;
- adoption/compliance requires staffed review, privacy/data limits and schedule/exit rules;
- AI-assisted option comparison maintains source/version/confidence but does not select urban form.

## Public-interest contract

Innovation space cannot displace anonymous public passage, existing residents/businesses, affordable ordinary work/community functions, vulnerable users, workers or living systems. Each pilot states who benefits, who bears disruption, what non-digital route remains, and what happens if the innovation programme closes.

## Owner contribution

Owner systems/AI expertise is `SUPPORTING`: useful for requirements, state/TTL, safety boundaries, observability, fallback and implementation metrics. It does not generate the urban concept. Urban design, transport, building, landscape, heritage, legal/title and public-engagement expertise remain independent dependencies.
