# Candidate 03 Spatial Framework v0.1

## Drawing rule

Every line and patch in the future plan must be labelled as one of:

- **verified existing condition** from an authoritative or cleared source;
- **derived relationship** with reproducible inputs;
- **provisional working geometry**;
- **conceptual intervention**.

The current framework provides topology and decision rules, not exact redlines, parcel boundaries, alignments, dimensions or statutory controls.

## 1. 43.6 km² coordinated research range

### A living-systems relationship map

The wide-scale drawing should contain five separate layers:

1. **verified innovation institutions and urban services** — universities, institutes, enterprises, professional services, transport and public facilities only where sources support them;
2. **living-system evidence** — topography, water, soil, canopy, open space, habitat, heat, wind and air-quality evidence with source, date and uncertainty;
3. **people and access** — residents, workers, students, service workers and visitors represented through cleared aggregate evidence rather than inferred surveillance;
4. **three areas and two wings** — relationship zones, not invented administrative boundaries;
5. **flows and dependencies** — knowledge, training, equipment, capital, maintenance, material recovery, public problem briefs and scenario learning.

The world-class ecosystem is organised around shared capability and stewardship:

- universities and institutes contribute knowledge, facilities and training where agreements exist;
- enterprises translate research into products, construction, landscape, mobility and public-service applications;
- capital and professional services help viable work move beyond pilots;
- talent is supported by everyday environmental quality and public life;
- compute and data serve explicit questions, with access and retention rules;
- communities and public bodies define problems and assess public value through authorised processes;
- maintenance teams contribute operational knowledge as equal innovation participants.

No participant or partnership is presented as committed in v0.1.

### Three areas and two wings

The three key areas do not form a fixed production pipeline:

- Zhongzhiyuan leads productive habitat, material cycles and controlled outdoor/indoor testing;
- AI Origin leads public learning, care and university–city habitat practice;
- Dazhongsi leads dense metropolitan comfort, arrival and enterprise exchange;
- the Technology Service Wing offers professional, capital, standards and market interfaces;
- the Scenario Empowerment Wing offers diverse public-life contexts for carefully governed application and feedback.

Each area can initiate a challenge and draw on the others. This prevents the environmental theme from becoming a narrow climate-tech industrial chain.

## 2. 11.4 km² overall design range

## Spatial structure: 一带 · 三基质 · 四横脉 · 多踏脚石

### 2.1 Heritage seed corridor

The heritage corridor should:

- conserve and interpret verified railway assets and histories;
- reuse eligible materials only after heritage and safety review;
- support walking, cycling, rest and seasonal public life;
- provide a continuous but varied succession landscape where feasible;
- hold nurseries, seed libraries, maintenance demonstration and cultural programmes;
- connect to, but not monopolise, the district's public-space and ecological systems.

It is not assumed to coincide exactly with current mapped park geometry.

### 2.2 Three habitat matrices

A matrix is the dominant relationship between buildings, ground, public life and living systems in an area—not a uniform land-use colour.

- **Productive habitat matrix:** porous research/engineering blocks, material and repair yards, sheltered outdoor work, drainage and robust planted edges.
- **Learning habitat matrix:** courtyards, public learning rooms, small gardens, quiet spaces, school/care interfaces and walkable campus–community edges.
- **Metropolitan climate matrix:** dense mixed-use thresholds, continuous shade, rain-safe arrival, clean-air and quiet refuges, planted roofs/terraces where feasible, and small ecological stepping stones.

### 2.3 Four ecological cross-meshes

Each cross-mesh is mapped by need and feasible connection rather than drawn at equal spacing.

1. **Shade and walking mesh** — accessible, shaded and winter-aware routes between transit, public rooms, daily services and open spaces.
2. **Rainfall and soil mesh** — protect or restore infiltration, safe overflow, soil volume and water-sensitive maintenance subject to technical study.
3. **Habitat and biodiversity mesh** — connect verified habitat patches with appropriate planting, light, noise and maintenance regimes.
4. **Public-health and refuge mesh** — ordinary rest, drinking water, toilets, quiet, cooling/warming and clean-air refuge distributed through accountable facilities.

### 2.4 Multi-level stepping stones

Potential stepping-stone types include:

- retained mature-tree clusters;
- courtyards and campus edges;
- pocket gardens and rain gardens;
- roofs and terraces capable of safe, maintainable greening;
- underused paved areas selectively depaved;
- building thresholds with shade, seating and water;
- public rooms that support weather refuge and ecological learning;
- railway-material gardens after heritage review.

Their locations are not predetermined. A stepping stone must have an ecological or public-comfort function, an operator and a maintenance path.

## 3. Main urban systems

### Industry and urban functions

- retain the official area roles but prevent mono-functional campuses;
- locate public research and enterprise interfaces at accessible thresholds;
- integrate daily services, learning, care, repair and food near work and transit;
- make landscape, construction and maintenance capability visible without defining the entire economy as “green AI”;
- protect non-commercial public use within key projects;
- use shared facilities and service agreements before duplicating specialised assets.

### Public-space system

The public-space hierarchy is:

1. heritage seed corridor;
2. three differentiated habitat commons;
3. four cross-meshes;
4. block and building stepping stones;
5. staffed or accountable public rooms for refuge, learning and help.

Continuity means a sequence of usable conditions, not an uninterrupted green band.

### Blue-green system

The blue-green drawing must start with verified catchments, soils, canopy and habitat. Conceptual actions then follow a protect–repair–connect–add order:

1. protect high-value existing living assets;
2. repair compacted soil, severed drainage and hostile edges;
3. connect patches across streets and blocks;
4. add new planting, water-sensitive systems or habitat only where maintenance and technical studies support it.

Blue-green proposals must not claim flood-control, temperature or biodiversity performance without baselines and professional modelling.

### Transport system

- rail and public transport provide inter-area access;
- an accessible walking network reaches transit, daily services, public rooms and habitat commons;
- cycling routes connect the three matrices and wider networks after safety study;
- street crossings prioritise shade, visibility, drainage and short exposure;
- parking is managed and consolidated only after demand, access and emergency needs are verified;
- curb and logistics plans protect pedestrians, ecological edges and ordinary deliveries;
- event and test logistics use time windows and recovery rules;
- emergency, maintenance and inclusive access remain possible in all landscape states.

### AI scenario space

AI scenarios occupy bounded decision loops rather than a continuous sensor field:

**season or living-system need → spatial substrate → responsible steward → non-AI baseline → bounded AI assistance → observed response → adjust or stop**

Potential test spaces are limited parts of the productive garden, learning habitat and metropolitan climate foyer. Ordinary public-space access is never conditional on participation.

### Urban renewal logic

The renewal sequence is:

1. retain functioning buildings and living assets;
2. repair public access, soil, water, shade and building edges;
3. reuse interiors and ground floors for missing functions;
4. selectively subtract barriers or excessive hardscape where lawful and justified;
5. add only what verified spatial and service deficits require;
6. replace only when safety, condition, heritage, carbon, programme and public-value review shows repair/reuse is inadequate.

## 5. Explicit non-claims

This framework does not establish:

- exact official boundaries or station relationships;
- existing ecological quality or contamination status;
- public ownership or access rights;
- hydrological, thermal, habitat or carbon performance;
- planning permission, funding, operating commitments or partner participation;
- suitability of any roof, soil, structure or utility for a proposed intervention.
