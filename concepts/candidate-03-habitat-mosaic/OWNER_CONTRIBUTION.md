## Where the Owner could change the core

### 1. Design for bad seasons and failed services

The Owner can challenge a landscape plan that only works in a rendered spring day. A bounded state analysis can ask:

- what remains accessible during heat, intense rain, winter conditions or poor air quality;
- what happens when a public refuge, lift, drain, irrigation system or digital service is unavailable;
- where people, maintenance teams and logistics move during recovery;
- which ecological areas need protection from peak events or crowds;
- what must remain manually inspectable and operable;
- which connections provide genuine redundancy.

If this work changes the location of refuges, crossings, maintenance space, storage, access or project sequence, it is a core contribution.

### 2. Make uncertainty spatial

The Owner can help express confidence without false precision:

- high-confidence assets are protected immediately;
- suspected but unverified systems become survey priorities;
- uncertain links remain search areas rather than project lines;
- interventions with high downside wait for professional evidence;
- low-regret access and maintenance repairs proceed first;
- every model claim carries an input, uncertainty and decision consequence.

### 3. Keep tests small and reversible

For microclimate, habitat observation and soil/water maintenance tests, the Owner can define:

- minimum information needed;
- reference observations and error classes;
- degraded operation and manual fallback;
- decision authority;
- stop and restoration triggers;
- evidence that would cause a spatial recommendation to change.

## Work that must be led elsewhere

Candidate 03 requires qualified external leadership in:

- landscape architecture and urban design;
- ecology, arboriculture and biodiversity;
- hydrology, drainage, soil and contamination;
- microclimate, public health and building/environmental engineering;
- heritage conservation;
- transport, accessibility and fire/life safety;
- community engagement and social impact;
- property, statutory planning, costing and long-term landscape operations.

The Owner must not convert technical confidence in systems analysis into domain authority in these fields.

## Suggested Owner work package

1. Help define evidence and uncertainty classes for the living-system atlas.
2. Perform bounded structured observation only after a transparent protocol exists.
3. Facilitate one cross-system state/failure workshop with domain professionals.
4. Translate its findings into changes to routes, refuges, maintenance spaces and phases.
5. Design the three test protocols with explicit non-AI baselines and stop conditions.
6. Build a lightweight evidence ledger; avoid a city-scale digital twin unless later justified.
7. Document which attractive technical ideas were rejected for lack of urban or ecological value.

## Fit verdict

**Overall fit: supporting fit, with two conditional core opportunities.**

This is the lowest direct fit of the three candidates to the Owner's embodied-intelligence and orchestration background. That is not a defect. Candidate 03 should continue only because its urban and landscape proposition is strong, not because the Owner can retrofit it with technical vocabulary.
