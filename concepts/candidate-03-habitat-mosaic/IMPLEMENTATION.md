# Candidate 03 Implementation v0.1

No schedule, budget, approval, operator, partner or performance outcome is confirmed. Phases describe dependencies and decision gates.

## Phase 0 — ground truth and admissibility

Before selecting projects:

- obtain authoritative boundaries and applicable planning controls when available;
- resolve the Dazhongsi location/station relationship;
- verify the heritage park relationship to the overall scope;
- inventory buildings, uses, public access, mature trees, soil, water, drainage, habitat, topography, utilities, microclimate, transport and public facilities;
- screen potential contamination and structural/roof constraints through qualified professionals;
- map ownership/operation only from cleared evidence;
- undertake bounded structured observation of shade, rest, weather, routes, thresholds and maintenance;
- identify affected users without collecting unnecessary personal data;
- compare the direction with current submissions and open PRs.

### Phase 0 gate

Stop Candidate 03 if evidence does not support a meaningful non-linear living-system structure or if a current peer already occupies the same structure more credibly.

## Phase 1 — protect and repair

Low-regret project families, subject to evidence:

1. protect verified mature living and heritage assets;
2. repair accessibility, crossings, rest, shade and public-service gaps;
3. correct small drainage, soil or edge failures after technical review;
4. establish manual inspection, maintenance and complaint paths;
5. open temporary public learning/refuge rooms through authorised agreements;
6. mark evidence gaps and test boundaries clearly;
7. stop harmful event, paving or maintenance practices where authority and evidence permit.

No AI procurement is necessary for Phase 1.

## Phase 2 — three habitat commons and cross-mesh pilots

- adapt or create the Productive Habitat Commons;
- adapt or create the Learning Habitat Common;
- establish a pilot Metropolitan Climate Foyer only after geometry verification;
- complete selected shade/walking and public-health/refuge links;
- pilot rainfall/soil or habitat links only with qualified design;
- establish operators and maintenance funding before opening;
- run no more than three bounded AI tests against physical/manual baselines.

### Phase 2 gate

Continue only if the projects improve ordinary access and living-system care without creating unacceptable maintenance, ecological, privacy or displacement burdens.

## Phase 3 — connect and reuse

- extend verified cross-mesh links across streets and ownership edges;
- reuse ground floors, courtyards and suitable roofs for missing functions;
- create stepping stones where ecological/public value and maintenance are credible;
- strengthen university, enterprise, professional and public-service interfaces;
- integrate material recovery and repair into renewal projects;
- add compact new construction only for verified deficits;
- formalise seasonal and degraded operating states.

## Phase 4 — mosaic stewardship

- maintain and revise the living-system network;
- compare intended and observed outcomes through qualified review;
- operate annual cultural and learning cycles;
- share controlled lessons with the wider Haidian innovation network;
- renew, replace or retire digital components;
- remove failed interventions and restore affected spaces;
- review whether the concept still has distinct value.

## Renewal project decision card

Each project requires:

- evidence label and source;
- spatial problem and affected users;
- retain/repair/reuse/subtract/add/replace decision;
- heritage, ecology, access and utility review;
- physical/manual baseline;
- owner and operator types;
- capital and ongoing maintenance implications;
- ordinary, degraded, failure and recovery states;
- public access and complaint route;
- AI/data need, if any;
- stop, removal and restoration condition.

| **Near term** | Phase 0 evidence; protect/repair; small public-service and maintenance improvements | authoritative/cleared baseline, professional review and accountable operator |

No calendar years are invented.

## Operating and funding directions

Potential mechanisms to test, not commitments:

- public or institutional public-space and maintenance budgets;
- reuse and facility operating agreements;
- development-linked public-realm obligations where lawful;
- research grants for bounded tests, separated from basic maintenance;
- enterprise or philanthropic support without exclusive branding/control;
- shared maintenance procurement with public reporting;
- event income capped and reinvested without displacing ordinary access.

Basic shade, accessibility, toilets, public help and maintenance must not depend on experimental funding.

## Metrics to define after baseline

- continuity and quality of accessible, shaded and weather-resilient routes;
- protected and repaired living assets by verified condition, not gross “green area” alone;
- soil/water/habitat maintenance completion and recurrence of failures;
- ordinary free public use across seasons and user groups;
- public-room/refuge reliability;
- event impact and restoration time;
- reuse versus demolition/new-build decisions;
- operator and maintenance reliability;
- test continuation, correction and withdrawal;
- number of spatial decisions actually changed by evidence.

Targets require baselines and professional agreement.

## Principal implementation risks

- official geometry or statutory controls invalidate the structure;
- contaminated soil, utilities or hydrology make proposed links infeasible;
- ecological ambition exceeds professional evidence;
- capital projects are funded but maintenance is not;
- public access conflicts with institutional or private control;
- seasonal installations replace durable urban repair;
- AI test funding distorts project priorities;
- dense urban needs are subordinated to a landscape image;
- intervention causes displacement or harms existing businesses/users;
- the proposal duplicates a stronger existing submission.

## Correction protocol

When evidence contradicts a claim:

1. record the contradiction and affected source;
2. remove or relabel the claim;
3. update spatial, programme, scenario and phase dependencies;
4. compare protection, repair, relocation, redesign and deletion;
5. obtain appropriate professional and Owner decisions;
6. preserve rejected reasoning for future comparison.
