# Candidate 03 Culture, Brand and Operations v0.1

## Cultural proposition

### Three histories, one practice of renewal

- **Jing-Zhang railway culture:** movement, engineering, labour, material traces, national connection and landscapes changed by infrastructure.
- **Zhongguancun innovation culture:** research, experimentation, entrepreneurship, open exchange, rapid learning and revision.
- **AI new culture:** transparent limits, accountable assistance, public-interest evaluation and the ability to stop systems that do not help.

Candidate 03 connects them through **cultivation**: infrastructures and ideas are not launched once and finished; they are observed, repaired, handed over and renewed across seasons.

### Narrative sequence

1. **Track / 轨:** verified railway traces and stories establish historical depth.
2. **Ground / 土:** soil, water, labour and maintenance reveal the conditions beneath innovation.
3. **Canopy / 荫:** shade and shelter make public life possible.
4. **Mosaic / 拼:** different neighbourhoods and habitats connect without becoming uniform.
5. **Stewardship / 养:** technology assists care, evidence and revision rather than replacing them.

The story avoids claiming that industrial heritage is “nature restored” or that AI can autonomously manage an ecosystem.

## Landmark directions

Landmarks are conceptual directions. Sites, heritage treatment, height, structure and delivery remain unverified.

### 1. 京张种子站 / Jing-Zhang Seed Station

A reused or new low-rise civic landscape structure associated with the heritage corridor. It could combine seed/material library, railway stories, maintenance workshop, public rest and a visible seasonal yard.

**Why it matters:** joins railway material culture to long-term stewardship.

**Risk:** becoming a themed visitor centre detached from real maintenance.

### 2. 共学冠庭 / Common Canopy Court

An accessible shaded learning landscape at AI Origin, combining a public classroom, intergenerational table, quiet grove, weather refuge and transparent seasonal observation.

**Why it matters:** makes learning and care spatial, not only programmatic.

**Risk:** over-programming, institutional enclosure or a costly spectacle canopy.

### 3. 都会气候门廊 / Metropolitan Climate Foyer

A networked arrival landmark at a verified Dazhongsi-area metropolitan threshold: shade, rain refuge, public help, cultural interpretation, ordinary commerce and quiet pockets rather than a single object.

**Why it matters:** gives metropolitan intensity a humane civic address.

**Risk:** the provisional geometry is wrong or the foyer becomes an event plaza.

### 4. 生产生境庭 / Productive Habitat Yard

An optional fourth landmark direction for Zhongzhiyuan: a robust public/technical interface showing repair, water, soil, material cycles and supervised work.

**Why it matters:** recognises maintenance and production as innovation culture.

**Risk:** staged demonstration without safe productive use.

At least three should be tested; none is approved.

## Logo and visual identity

### Core mark

A modular mark made from three unequal tiles crossed by one fine railway trace. The gaps between tiles form a leaf, water path or walking connection depending on scale. It should read as an urban mosaic before it reads as a technology brand.

### Visual grammar

- **base:** warm mineral/railway grey for inherited material;
- **living-system family:** restrained soil, water and canopy tones;
- **seasonal accent:** one changing colour for annual programmes;
- **AI layer:** a fine dotted or measured line, never the dominant luminous motif;
- **shape:** irregular but compatible tiles, avoiding generic leaf, circuit-brain or robot icons;
- **type:** legible bilingual typography suitable for wayfinding and technical evidence.

Exact colours, fonts and accessibility contrast require professional design.

### Spatial application

- heritage markers identify source and uncertainty;
- ground/edge markers show stewardship and maintenance responsibility;
- four cross-mesh symbols distinguish shade, water/soil, habitat and refuge;
- test areas use a temporary, clearly bounded visual layer;
- non-AI routes and services are marked at equal prominence;
- commercial branding cannot overwrite public-space or heritage information.

## Annual activity system

### Spring — Seed and Repair Week

- railway-material and planting stewardship;
- soil and drainage learning;
- public repair workshops;
- professional review of proposed living-system interventions.

### Summer — Shade and Night Commons

- heat-safe evening walks and culture;
- shaded-route/accessibility review;
- ordinary-service night openings;
- controlled microclimate test reporting.

### Autumn — Mosaic Open Studios

- research, enterprise, landscape, maintenance and public-service demonstrations;
- cross-neighbourhood learning;
- material reuse and construction practice;
- public review of what changed spatial decisions.

### Winter — Ground Ledger Assembly

- publish evidence, maintenance failures and corrected assumptions;
- compare manual and AI-assisted scenarios;
- review budgets, operators and next-year withdrawals;
- quiet indoor public learning rather than outdoor spectacle.

Events never prove year-round performance.

## Long-term operations

### Stewardship cells

Each major commons needs an accountable stewardship cell comprising relevant operator types: public-space/facility management, maintenance, accessibility, heritage/ecology expertise and public user representation through authorised mechanisms. Exact bodies are unknown.

### Mosaic forum

A periodic cross-area forum compares:

- living-system and public-use evidence;
- maintenance and access failures;
- event impacts and restoration;
- project continuation or removal;
- test outcomes and data burdens;
- conflicts between local needs and district branding.

### Public evidence

Publish only cleared, aggregated and decision-relevant evidence:

- project purpose and status;
- responsible organisation type and public contact;
- maintenance/service hours;
- known limitations and failures;
- test baseline, outcome and stop decision;
- changes to assumptions or spatial proposals.

Do not publish personal, proprietary or sensitive ecological/location data.

## Cultural and brand risks

- generic eco-city imagery erases railway and working history;
- “habitat” becomes a marketing word without ecological expertise;
- seasonal events damage the spaces they celebrate;
- maintenance labour becomes invisible;
- AI is reintroduced as spectacle through media installations;
- irregular mosaic graphics hide a weak plan;
- heritage materials are moved or reused without authority;
- a logo is mistaken for evidence of a coherent landscape system.
