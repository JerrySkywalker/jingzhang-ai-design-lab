# Candidate 03 Urban Systems v0.1

All quantities, locations and performance targets remain open until authoritative plans, field evidence and professional studies exist.

## 1. Land-use strategy

### Principle

Land use follows a living-systems and daily-life diagnosis, not a green colour overlay.

1. protect verified heritage, mature living assets, functioning public services and viable productive/community uses;
2. identify missing daily functions and spatial conflicts;
3. use mixed-use edges and shared facilities to connect research, enterprise and ordinary life;
4. preserve ground for soil, water, shade, public access, maintenance and logistics;
5. reserve bounded test space without turning whole neighbourhoods into test beds;
6. add development only where reuse and operational change cannot meet a verified need.

### Functional distribution

- **Zhongzhiyuan:** research/engineering, shared testing and repair, productive habitat, training and worker daily services.
- **AI Origin:** university–city learning, entrepreneurship, community/care interfaces, quiet life and cultivated public space.
- **Dazhongsi:** enterprise/professional exchange, metropolitan commerce and culture, public arrival/refuge and local daily services.
- **Heritage seed corridor:** heritage, movement, ecology, learning and distributed maintenance; not a speculative development strip.
- **Cross-meshes and stepping stones:** public, ecological and infrastructure relationships across multiple land uses.

No parcel allocation or development intensity is claimed.

## 2. Buildings: retain, renew, reuse and add

### Decision ladder

For each building or structure:

1. verify identity, condition, current use, heritage value, ownership constraints and access;
2. retain if it performs a viable function and can meet essential safety/access needs;
3. repair envelope, services, accessibility and climate response;
4. reuse interiors, ground floors, roofs or yards for a verified missing function;
5. selectively subtract barriers, redundant pavement or unsafe appendages after review;
6. add compact infill or lightweight structures only with a clear public/functional need;
7. replace only after safety, heritage, whole-life carbon, service continuity and public-value comparison.

### Massing and urban character

- keep a varied inherited skyline and block grain where viable;
- shape additions around daylight, ventilation, tree/soil space, drainage, public thresholds and existing context;
- use lower or stepped edges beside habitat commons and sensitive fabric where evidence supports it;
- avoid a continuous “green megastructure,” spectacle canopy or homogeneous eco-tech façade;
- treat roofs as technical surfaces first; public or ecological use requires structure, access, fire safety and maintenance proof;
- express repair, material reuse and seasonal change honestly;
- use new landmarks as civic landscapes or shared structures, not automatically tall buildings.

Height, FAR, density, green ratio and setbacks remain unknown.

## 3. Mobility and logistics

### Rail and transit

- connect each matrix to verified rail and bus access;
- clarify arrival, transfer, accessibility and late-hour routes;
- improve first/last-mile comfort through shade, rain protection, rest and legibility;
- never claim a station relationship until official geometry is resolved.

### Streets and roads

- keep necessary emergency, service and general traffic access;
- prioritise short, visible crossings at public and ecological links;
- use traffic calming or reallocation only after movement, safety and access study;
- integrate drainage and tree soil without creating unsafe edges;
- manage events and tests as temporary states with restoration duties.

### Walking and accessibility

- provide continuous step-free routes among transit, daily services, public rooms and habitat commons;
- combine physical wayfinding, shade/rest intervals and staffed help;
- audit night, rain, heat, winter and construction states;
- preserve non-digital access.

### Cycling

- connect the three matrices and wider network;
- separate fast through cycling from crowded habitat commons where needed;
- provide secure parking near destinations without blocking public space;
- include repair and charging only where safe and operated.

### Parking, curb and logistics

- verify demand before reducing or consolidating parking;
- preserve accessible and essential service parking;
- use shared/consolidated parking only if ownership and walking access work;
- define delivery, ride-hail, event, research and maintenance curb zones;
- separate hazardous or heavy technical logistics from public routes;
- schedule logistics robustly but retain human control and contingency space.

## 4. Jing-Zhang heritage park and blue-green system

### Heritage seed corridor

Railway history is carried through verified artifacts, alignments, working stories, material culture and movement. Landscape succession is used as a contemporary layer, not to erase industrial character.

Potential elements:

- conserved railway structures and traces;
- reused sleepers, steel or aggregate only when lawful and safe;
- chronological planting and maintenance stories;
- seed and material libraries;
- walking/cycling and rest;
- small learning and stewardship spaces;
- connections from the corridor into surrounding blocks.

### Blue-green decision sequence

- **protect:** verified mature trees, viable soils, drainage paths, habitat and water assets;
- **repair:** compaction, poor rooting conditions, severed flows and hostile edges;
- **connect:** cross-street, block-edge and roof/courtyard stepping stones;
- **add:** planting, water-sensitive systems and habitat designed for the real context;
- **maintain:** fund inspection, care, replacement and seasonal adaptation;
- **learn:** compare intended and observed outcomes without overstating causation.

Hydrology, contamination, utility, species and climate studies are prerequisites to performance claims.

## 5. Public-space system

### Public-space promise

Every major public-space project must support:

- free and non-commercial use;
- accessible movement and rest;
- weather protection or seasonal comfort;
- drinking water, toilets or clear access to them at appropriate centres;
- legible responsibility and maintenance;
- quiet and unscripted time;
- event and test recovery;
- safe non-AI operation.

### Hierarchy

1. heritage seed corridor;
2. Productive, Learning and Metropolitan habitat commons;
3. shade/walking, rainfall/soil, habitat/biodiversity and health/refuge cross-meshes;
4. block/courtyard/roof stepping stones;
5. staffed public rooms and maintenance bases.

## 6. Municipal and new infrastructure

- coordinate water, drainage, energy, communications, waste, fire and emergency systems with living-system projects;
- protect utility access from planting or public-space conflicts;
- use distributed water-sensitive interventions only with catchment and maintenance proof;
- prioritise low-energy shade, ventilation, material and operating measures before automated control;
- integrate repair, material sorting and landscape maintenance facilities;
- use interoperable, replaceable digital components for limited scenarios;
- define ordinary, degraded, failure and recovery states;
- retain manual inspection and public reporting paths.

## 7. AI innovation ecosystem

### Relationship logic

**public/living-system need → verified spatial condition → multidisciplinary question → physical baseline → bounded data need → model or service → accountable operator → public/technical review → spatial or operating adjustment**

Universities, researchers, companies, maintainers, public bodies, communities, capital and service partners can take different roles. None is treated as committed without evidence. AI enterprises need not all be environmental specialists; the district supplies high-quality public-interest problems and shared evaluation discipline across a broad ecosystem.

### Test contract

Each test must define:

- public question and affected users;
- bounded place and duration;
- authoritative human decision-maker;
- physical and non-AI baseline;
- minimum data and prohibited inferences;
- reference or ground-truth method;
- success, error, stop and withdrawal criteria;
- safety, accessibility, privacy and complaint paths;
- restoration and evidence-retention responsibilities.

No benchmark result is claimed in v0.1.

## 8. User profiles

At least these groups must shape plans and tests:

1. **researcher or engineer** needing reliable work, collaboration, food, rest and transport;
2. **maintenance, landscape or service worker** needing safe access, storage, facilities and respected operational knowledge;
3. **resident caregiver with child** needing everyday routes, toilets, shade, quiet and non-commercial space;
4. **older or disabled person** needing step-free, rested, legible and non-digital access;
5. **student or early-career worker** needing affordable food, learning, work and evening public life;
6. **local merchant or small enterprise** needing predictable access, deliveries and ordinary footfall;
7. **commuter or metropolitan visitor** needing short transfer, weather refuge, directions and public help;
8. **ecologist, heritage or public-realm professional** needing traceable evidence and the authority to reject harmful technical ideas.

These are design lenses, not claims about actual demographics.

## 9. Cross-system failure modes

- landscape intervention conflicts with buried utilities or emergency access;
- “smart” maintenance continues after physical assets or operators fail;
- events compact soil, damage habitat or displace ordinary use;
- flood/heat claims exceed engineering evidence;
- roof/terrace proposals exceed structural or operational capacity;
- ecological protection becomes exclusionary or inaccessible;
- sensors create privacy, bias or maintenance burdens;
- AI advice is mistaken for professional safety or ecological judgment;
- cross-mesh links stop at ownership boundaries;
- long-term maintenance loses funding after capital construction.

Design response must change space, responsibility or phasing—not merely add warnings.
