# Candidate 03 Three Key Areas v0.1

**FACT:** The official working brief lists approximate areas of 192.1 ha for Zhongzhiyuan, 104.3 ha for Beijing AI Origin Community and 72 ha for Dazhongsi.

**Caution:** Exact official polygons and controls are unavailable. The designs below are typological hypotheses, not site plans or statutory claims.

## 1. Zhongzhiyuan — Productive Habitat District

### Positioning

A productive urban district where research, engineering, repair and controlled testing coexist with robust soils, water management, shade, habitat and worker daily life. Ecology is working infrastructure, not a landscaped buffer around laboratories.

### Spatial structure

**Productive commons + porous work blocks + repair/water yards + habitat edges**

- a publicly legible productive habitat common;
- existing work and research blocks opened selectively at safe thresholds;
- separate public, technical, emergency and logistics paths;
- a sequence of sheltered outdoor work, learning and rest spaces;
- water/soil and habitat connections across rather than only along the railway;
- small stepping stones in yards, edges and suitable roofs.

Exact alignments await verified parcel, building, access and environmental data.

### Function layout

- research, engineering and enterprise space;
- shared prototyping, repair, material-learning and training facilities;
- bounded environmental/urban-systems test spaces;
- public interpretation and supervised learning;
- food, rest, basic retail, worker support and health/refuge services;
- maintenance, storage and logistics integrated as real programme;
- non-commercial public space protected from test operations.

### Buildings and renewal

- retain sound research, industrial and service buildings;
- audit structural, environmental, heritage and operational condition before intervention;
- reuse selected ground floors and bays for shared facilities and public interfaces;
- repair roofs, drainage, thresholds and shaded edges;
- selectively remove unsafe barriers or redundant hardscape only with evidence;
- add compact buildings only for verified missing functions;
- avoid cosmetic “eco-industrial” façades and uncontrolled rooftop greening.

### Public space

The Productive Habitat Commons combines an outdoor work/learning terrace, robust planted ground, shade, rest, public interpretation and visible maintenance. Public access, technical work and testing are spatially and temporally separated.

### Mobility and active travel

- accessible routes connect transit, daily services and the commons;
- walking/cycling crossings prioritise visibility and shade;
- freight and hazardous/technical logistics have defined paths and time windows;
- parking and loading do not sever soil/water connections;
- test vehicles or robots never claim ordinary pedestrian space by default;
- emergency access remains clear in wet, event and maintenance states.

### AI scenarios

- controlled comparison of microclimate prediction and measured comfort;
- minimum-sensing support for soil/water maintenance;
- scheduling of shared test space with manual override;
- material and repair passport assistance;
- accessible route information during works or weather events.

AI does not autonomously control public access, safety or ecological treatment.

### Daily life

Workers and visitors need affordable food, drinking water, toilets, shade, quiet rest, first aid, non-digital help, evening routes and public transport access. Nearby residents need protection from noise, light, freight and test spillover. Ordinary conditions govern the plan more than demonstration days.

### Implementation risks

- contaminated soil or constrained utilities invalidate landscape assumptions;
- safety and IP requirements limit public permeability;
- productive ecology becomes branding around sealed campuses;
- technical tests damage public access or habitat;
- maintenance responsibilities and budgets are absent;
- freight, emergency and ecological networks conflict;
- the assumed area context changes with official geometry.

## 2. Beijing AI Origin Community — Learning Habitat Community

### Positioning

An intergenerational university–city learning habitat where research culture, entrepreneurship, public learning, care and everyday neighbourhood life share cultivated ground and accessible public rooms.

### Spatial structure

**Learning garden + campus–community rooms + quiet-life web + habitat courtyards**

- a central learning habitat common linked to the heritage seed corridor;
- public rooms at verified campus/community thresholds;
- a walking network connecting transit, learning, care, food and rest;
- courtyards and small gardens as ecological and social stepping stones;
- quiet and refuge spaces protected from event pressure;
- cross-mesh links for shade, rainfall, habitat and public health.

### Function layout

- university/research and entrepreneurship interfaces;
- public learning, exhibition, workshop and mentoring rooms;
- childcare, older-person, accessibility and community-service interfaces subject to actual needs;
- small-team and shared work spaces;
- food, daily retail, exercise, quiet study and non-commercial seating;
- nursery, seed/maintenance learning and citizen-observation programmes;
- housing-support relationships examined but not invented as new housing entitlement.

### Buildings and renewal

- retain viable campus, community and heritage fabric;
- open selected ground floors only through negotiated, managed access;
- reuse underused rooms for learning and care-related functions;
- improve daylight, ventilation, accessibility, thresholds and courtyards;
- protect mature trees and adequate soil volumes where verified;
- use additions sparingly to complete a real public or service gap;
- avoid treating all courtyards as event venues or sensor laboratories.

### Public space

The Learning Habitat Common is a sequence of garden rooms: public classroom, quiet grove, intergenerational table, seasonal yard and weather refuge. Its schedule preserves unscripted use and recovery periods.

### Mobility and active travel

- safe accessible walking between transit, campus/community thresholds and daily services;
- continuous shade where ecologically and technically appropriate;
- cycle access and parking outside primary pedestrian rooms;
- managed school/care, delivery and event curb periods if those uses are verified;
- clear routes that do not require a phone or institutional badge;
- event dispersal that protects neighbourhood quiet.

### AI scenarios

- public explanation of seasonal and habitat evidence;
- bounded habitat-observation benchmark with expert ground truth;
- accessible route assistance;
- voluntary learning tools that do not infer identity or performance;
- shared-room and garden scheduling with a staffed alternative.

### Daily life

Students, residents, children, older people, caregivers, service workers, researchers and visitors should find drinking water, toilets, affordable food, shade, quiet, seating, public help and evening safety. Participation in research or data collection is never a condition of access.

### Implementation risks

- campus/community access cannot be negotiated;
- public learning becomes promotional display;
- “citizen science” extracts unpaid data or misstates expertise;
- programmes displace ordinary quiet use;
- care or housing-support claims lack authority and operators;
- planting and public rooms lack maintenance;
- the assumed boundary does not contain the relevant institutions or communities.

## 3. Dazhongsi — Metropolitan Climate Foyer

### Positioning

A dense metropolitan arrival and exchange district where shade, rain safety, clean-air and quiet refuge, legible public thresholds, commerce and cultural life make station-area intensity humane.

### Geometry warning

**FACT:** Official-repository Issue #1029 reports that provisional key-area geometry is not anchored to Dazhongsi Station and appears closer to Beijing North Railway Station. This concept therefore does not claim an exact station, boundary or site. “Metropolitan arrival” is a required hypothesis for verification, not a mapped fact.

### Spatial structure

**Climate foyer + shaded arrival web + public thresholds + compact stepping stones**

- a metropolitan climate foyer at a verified high-access threshold;
- shaded and rain-safe walking links to transit, daily services and the heritage seed corridor;
- public ground-floor or sheltered spaces for rest, help and exchange;
- small planted, water-sensitive and quiet stepping stones fitted to dense fabric;
- managed commercial, event and logistics edges;
- cultural interpretation integrated with ordinary movement.

### Function layout

- enterprise exchange and professional services;
- small-team work, meeting, training and exhibition;
- metropolitan retail, food, repair and everyday services;
- cultural and railway-heritage interpretation;
- staffed public help, toilets, water, rest and weather refuge;
- small climate/health learning and test functions;
- neighbourhood-serving uses protected from event-only conversion.

### Buildings and renewal

- retain viable dense mixed-use fabric and active local businesses;
- improve accessibility, shade, drainage, ventilation and legibility at thresholds;
- reuse selected interiors and ground floors for public/shared functions;
- add canopies, arcades or compact structures only after heritage, fire, structure and public-space review;
- use suitable roofs/terraces as stepping stones only with structural and maintenance proof;
- selective replacement requires an evidence-backed public-value case;
- avoid a singular iconic tower or sealed convention complex.

### Public space

The Metropolitan Climate Foyer is a network, not one plaza: shaded arrival, rain refuge, quiet pocket, cultural threshold, public table and late-hour safe route. It must remain useful between events and without consumption.

### Mobility and active travel

- prioritise accessible transit arrival and short, legible crossings;
- separate through movement, waiting and event queues;
- integrate cycling and parking without occupying refuge spaces;
- manage taxis, ride-hail, deliveries and event loading through verified curb plans;
- preserve emergency and maintenance access;
- provide non-digital wayfinding and staffed assistance;
- use seasonal/event states with clear recovery to ordinary use.

### AI scenarios

- accessible and weather-aware route assistance with physical wayfinding fallback;
- bounded footfall/comfort comparison using privacy-protective methods;
- maintenance triage for shade, drainage and refuge assets;
- event capacity support with a human safety authority;
- enterprise/public-space information without identity inference.

### Daily life

Commuters, service workers, local residents, older people, disabled users, visitors and small merchants need short transfers, affordable food, rest, toilets, water, quiet, clear directions and reliable late-hour routes. The foyer cannot be reserved for professional events.

### Implementation risks

- official geometry invalidates the station-area premise;
- shade/rain structures conflict with heritage, utilities, fire access or ownership;
- high footfall overwhelms small refuge spaces;
- event commerce displaces local services;
- environmental performance is claimed without measurement;
- dense-fabric greening is structurally or operationally infeasible;
- fragmented ownership prevents a coherent public network.

## Shared completeness test

The three areas are not copies:

| Area | Dominant habitat problem | Main public-space type | Primary innovation interface | Critical conflict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zhongzhiyuan | Productive ground, water, shade and safe work/public coexistence | Productive Habitat Commons | Research–engineering–repair–test | Logistics/test safety versus public and ecological continuity |
| AI Origin | Learning, care, quiet and campus–community permeability | Learning Habitat Common | University–city learning and entrepreneurship | Institutional access/event pressure versus ordinary life |
| Dazhongsi | Dense arrival comfort, refuge and metropolitan thresholds | Metropolitan Climate Foyer network | Enterprise and public metropolitan exchange | Footfall/event/logistics intensity versus refuge and local use |

All three must still address work, learning, care, rest, mobility, maintenance and ordinary public access in locally appropriate proportions.
