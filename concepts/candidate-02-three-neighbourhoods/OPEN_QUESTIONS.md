# Candidate 02 Open Questions and Kill Criteria

## Official evidence gaps

1. What are the authoritative polygons for the overall and three key-area scopes?
2. Where is Dazhongsi key area in relation to Dazhongsi Station, Beijing North Railway Station and the provisional geometry?
3. Which parts of the mapped Jing-Zhang heritage park actually intersect the official design scope?
4. What are the applicable land-use, FAR, height, density, green-ratio, setback, heritage and transport controls?
5. What buildings, ground floors, facilities and public routes exist, and what are their ownership and access conditions?
6. What are the actual resident, worker, student, visitor and service-worker patterns across the day and week?
7. Which collaboration relationships among universities, institutes, companies, capital and public bodies are documented rather than assumed?

## Design questions

1. Are there three legible neighbourhood catchments, or is “three” merely inherited from the required detailed-design areas?
2. What necessary daily function is missing in each area, and can it be supplied by reuse?
3. How can local completeness avoid inefficient duplication while preserving resilience?
4. What makes the Making, Learning and Metropolitan neighbourhoods spatially different rather than programmatically relabelled?
5. Which links among the three neighbourhoods are important enough to draw, and which are speculative?
6. Can the heritage park operate as a common without becoming another dominant linear spine?
7. How are affordability, public access and service-worker needs protected without authority the design team does not possess?
8. Which ordinary-life improvements remain valuable if no AI system is procured?
9. What accountable institutions could operate the three civic centres?
10. How does the proposal avoid making existing communities perform as scenery for an innovation brand?

## Owner decisions required

- Is a familiar but potentially strong complete-neighbourhood proposition worth carrying against a crowded field?
- Is the team prepared to fund the urban, social, accessibility and operating expertise needed to make it credible?
- Should “three complete neighbourhoods” remain the main structure if field evidence produces more or fewer meaningful catchments?
- How much local duplication is acceptable for daily-life resilience?
- Which three bounded AI tests, if any, deserve further development?

## Kill criteria

Candidate 02 should be stopped or fundamentally reconstituted if any of these conditions persists after the next evidence round:

1. a high-quality existing submission already expresses the same federation of complete neighbourhoods with equal or greater spatial specificity;
2. the proposal cannot produce a clear, diagrammable 11.4 km² structure that is not three beads on the railway;
3. the three key areas become copy-pasted civic centres with different names;
4. “complete neighbourhood” means only added amenities around Candidate 01's three specialist engines;
5. verified daily-life, building and facility evidence does not support three meaningful catchments;
6. the Jing-Zhang heritage relationship is generic, decorative or interchangeable with any linear park;
7. public value is framed mainly around elite AI talent rather than residents, service workers, children, older people and ordinary visitors;
8. AI additions displace human access, accountable operators or robust non-AI service;
9. the concept depends on unknown policy powers, unverified partners, widespread demolition or large new construction;
10. no plausible operator, maintenance responsibility or ordinary-day funding path can be identified;
11. the federation is only an organisational diagram and does not change land use, mobility, public space or phasing;
12. professional and field review finds that Candidate 02 is less site-specific than the closest “stay,” “belong,” “living meridian” or “local unit” competitors.

## Evidence that would justify continuation

- verified and meaningfully different facility/service deficits in all three areas;
- three distinct figure-ground and public-space prototypes;
- a credible map of local catchments and inter-neighbourhood exchange;
- reuse-first projects tied to known buildings or spatial conditions;
- an operating model with named responsibility types and non-digital access;
- a collision review showing more than vocabulary-level difference.
