# Candidate 02 Owner Contribution Analysis

The Owner's technical background is an evaluation dimension, not the source of this concept. Candidate 02 should remain credible if every aerospace, embodied-intelligence and multi-agent reference is removed.

## Where the Owner could be unusually valuable

### 1. Completeness under non-ideal conditions

Many “complete neighbourhood” proposals describe only normal daytime operation. The Owner can make a stronger contribution by testing:

- a lift or accessible route outage;
- a civic room closing unexpectedly;
- rain, heat or poor air quality;
- evening and weekend service gaps;
- an event displacing ordinary use;
- digital booking or identification failure;
- a logistics conflict at a public threshold;
- local overload and cross-neighbourhood assistance.

The result should be changed routes, redundant services, staffed fallback, protected time windows or phased investments—not merely a risk table.

### 2. Federation without central control

The three neighbourhoods need a precise answer to what is local, what is shared and what happens when a shared service fails. The Owner can help define:

- local decision rights for ordinary public-space and facility operation;
- common data and service standards that do not require one dominant platform;
- opt-in exchange of rooms, equipment, training and event capacity;
- escalation and recovery paths;
- public evidence for continuation, adjustment or withdrawal.

### 3. Small, falsifiable test design

The Owner can help make three bounded tests genuinely testable: accessible-route assistance, shared-space allocation and local curb/logistics coordination. Each needs a baseline, non-AI comparator, defined users, stop condition and accountable human operator.

## Contributions that require other professionals

The Owner is not a substitute for:

- lead urban design and landscape architecture;
- transport, accessibility and public-realm design;
- housing, public-service and anti-displacement expertise;
- heritage conservation;
- community engagement and social research;
- real-estate, property-rights and operating feasibility;
- statutory planning, municipal and fire/life-safety review.

Candidate 02 becomes weaker if technical precision is used to mask missing urban, social or institutional competence.

## Practical Owner work package

1. Prepare an evidence-labelled ordinary-day observation protocol.
2. Select no more than three service chains for dependency and failure-mode mapping.
3. Test whether the resulting findings change the three neighbourhood plans.
4. Help define the three bounded AI tests and their non-AI baselines.
5. Maintain a decision ledger separating facts, derived findings, assumptions, concepts and Owner decisions.
6. Stop technical work that does not improve spatial, daily-life or operational decisions.

## Fit verdict

**Overall fit: supporting-to-moderate core fit.**

The Owner can materially improve the federation logic and reliability of everyday services. The Owner's strongest technical backgrounds are not the concept's source of distinctiveness, and that is acceptable. Candidate 02 should be judged primarily as an urban neighbourhood proposition.
