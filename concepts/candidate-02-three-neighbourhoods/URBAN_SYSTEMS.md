# Candidate 02 Urban Systems v0.1

## 1. Land and function

### Strategy

The candidate uses a **completeness-before-specialisation** test.

Each neighbourhood receives a different innovation emphasis, but every one must provide a defensible mix of productive, civic, daily-life, cultural and green functions. No numerical allocation is frozen before official land-use, building, population and facility data are available.

Conceptual priorities:

- preserve established housing, education, research and public-service functions unless evidence supports change;
- locate active public and enterprise interfaces at verified accessible centres and streets;
- protect quiet interiors from testing, events and logistics;
- provide small and medium adaptable spaces alongside large institutional floors;
- treat public space and blue-green systems as structure, not residual land;
- prevent each specialist cluster from exporting all care, food, maintenance and service labour to another district.

## 2. Buildings and urban character

### Retain, renew, reuse and necessary new build

| Action | Conceptual trigger | Evidence required before a specific decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain | useful building, stable community/service use, heritage or embodied value | survey, structure, use, ownership, heritage and user evidence |
| Repair | access, envelope, energy, fire, servicing or public-interface deficit | technical audit and operating responsibility |
| Reuse | space can support a missing neighbourhood function | structure, fire, access, lease/ownership and fit-out feasibility |
| Add | reuse cannot meet a verified need or a reversible addition enables testing | planning, structure, fire, municipal and public-realm review |
| Replace | retention is unsafe or prevents a demonstrable public outcome | full professional and legal process; not decided here |

### Massing and character

- local centres should be enclosed by human-scaled, active and permeable edges;
- larger research buildings need visible public fronts and separate servicing;
- residential, school, care, heritage and ecological edges need calmer massing and activity;
- additions should be legible as contemporary work without imitating railway heritage;
- the skyline should express three different urban districts rather than one continuous branded frontage;
- screens and luminous façades are not the default expression of AI.

## 3. Mobility system

### Rail and public transport

- use existing rail/transit as the primary connection among the three neighbourhoods;
- design station-to-neighbourhood arrival as a complete sequence;
- do not assume new lines, station entrances or capacity;
- retain staffed and static information as the fallback.

### Roads and curb

- diagnose through-traffic and local access separately;
- give civic centres pedestrian-priority design where traffic evidence allows;
- use managed curb zones for loading, pick-up, mobility assistance and events;
- locate technical and large deliveries at productive edges;
- do not claim road-redline or junction engineering.

### Walking, accessibility and cycling

- map a five-minute accessible web in each neighbourhood;
- prioritise continuity, shade, seating, crossings, toilets and help points;
- connect cycling routes to secure parking and end-of-trip facilities;
- audit the route as a chain: a single inaccessible link invalidates the claim;
- compare day, night, weekday, weekend and event conditions.

### Parking and logistics

- inventory existing supply and use before proposing quantities;
- share parking where schedules and ownership allow;
- avoid parking fronts on key civic spaces;
- create neighbourhood-specific logistics plans;
- require every AI/robotic logistics test to yield to ordinary walking and manual service.

## 4. Municipal and new infrastructure

Candidate 02 proposes no confirmed capacity or routing. The later professional package should test:

- water, wastewater, drainage, power, heating/cooling, solid waste and emergency access;
- distributed public toilets, drinking water and maintenance stores;
- secure compute/data infrastructure located in controlled buildings;
- shared event power/network interfaces that do not become permanent clutter;
- service continuity during power, network or AI failure;
- maintenance access designed with the public realm.

## 5. Heritage park and blue-green system

The Jing-Zhang heritage park is the common landscape between neighbourhoods. It should:

- preserve and explain railway history with verified content;
- maintain ordinary daily use before events and AI scenarios;
- connect local green spaces without asserting unverified hydrology;
- support shade, rest, accessibility and seasonal comfort;
- include quiet and low-light portions;
- expose maintenance responsibilities without turning the park into an operations display.

Local green systems in each neighbourhood should be at least as important as the common park. A resident should not need to reach the linear park for every form of outdoor life.

## 6. Public-space component families

1. neighbourhood civic centre;
2. small public courtyard or garden;
3. shared ground-floor room;
4. station/arrival forecourt;
5. research/public threshold;
6. quiet rest and sensory refuge;
7. local play/exercise space;
8. staffed help and maintenance point.

Components are selected by local need, not repeated as branded furniture.

## 7. AI innovation ecosystem

### Parallel local loops

The candidate rejects a mandatory north-to-south innovation pipeline. Each neighbourhood can complete a smaller loop:

- identify a local or industry problem;
- assemble knowledge, service and people;
- prototype or adapt;
- test in a bounded setting;
- review with users and professional owners;
- continue, change, relocate or stop.

Neighbourhoods exchange capability when needed:

- Zhongzhiyuan contributes engineering and controlled validation;
- AI Origin contributes research translation, learning and social feedback;
- Dazhongsi contributes enterprise services, market/public interface and metropolitan communication;
- both wings contribute external services and scenario contexts.

### Eight resources

Land, space, capital, talent, compute, data, scenarios and public trust are treated as separate resources with separate owners and access conditions. No fictional “one platform” is assumed to control them all.

## 8. Unified AI scenario generation logic

Every scenario follows:

**neighbourhood user → ordinary need → responsible institution → place and human service → bounded AI enhancement → minimum data → failure/exit → public-value review**

Removing AI must leave a usable basic service. If removing AI collapses the service, the scenario is not ready.

### Three test directions

1. **Interoperability:** can different edge/AI systems use a common, bounded test protocol without proprietary public infrastructure?
2. **Maintainability:** can faults be identified, explained and repaired by accountable humans within a declared service window?
3. **Public adoption:** does a product/service provide measurable benefit at a metropolitan public interface compared with a human/manual baseline?

These are test directions, not approved operations.

## 10. User profiles

At minimum:

1. research engineer or technical worker;
2. early-stage founder or small team;
3. resident family or caregiver;
4. older person or disabled user;
5. student, learner or career changer;
6. maintenance, logistics, retail or service worker;
7. visitor, investor or conference participant.

Personas must later be replaced or corrected by real, ethical research. They are not claims about current residents.

## 11. System failure modes

- local completeness is asserted from a generic checklist rather than real gaps;
- three areas receive identical space/program mixes;
- local services cannot be funded or operated;
- innovation uses inflate costs and displace ordinary users;
- the park is still treated as the sole public realm;
- event and testing logistics overrun neighbourhood life;
- AI scenarios multiply without a responsible institution.
