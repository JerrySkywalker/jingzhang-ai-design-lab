# Candidate 02 Implementation v0.1

No schedule, budget, operator, approval or investment is confirmed. Phases are conceptual dependency groups.

## Phase 0 — neighbourhood evidence

Before selecting projects:

- replace provisional boundaries when authoritative data becomes available;
- verify the three areas’ actual location, context and station relationships;
- inventory land use, buildings, ground-floor access, housing-support, daily services, public space, mobility and logistics;
- map ownership/operation only from cleared sources;
- undertake structured, non-personal field observation;
- speak with residents, workers and relevant professional stakeholders through an authorised process;
- identify neighbourhood deficits and assets;
- recheck peer proposals and collision risk.

### Phase 0 decision gate

Stop Candidate 02 if the evidence does not support three meaningful and distinct neighbourhood catchments.

## Phase 1 — ordinary-day repair

Low-regret project families:

1. accessibility and walking continuity repair;
2. toilets, water, seating, shade and staffed help gaps;
3. local wayfinding and transit-arrival clarification;
4. reversible reuse of selected public/service rooms;
5. cycling parking and curb/logistics pilots;
6. quiet-space and non-consumption public-use protection;
7. public service and learning pilots with non-AI equivalents.

Success is measured on ordinary weekdays and evenings, not only launch events.

## Phase 2 — three civic centres and local webs

Subject to evidence and agreement:

- adapt or create the Making Commons;
- adapt or create the Origin Learning House;
- adapt or create the Metropolitan Exchange Hall;
- complete each centre’s five-minute access web;
- formalise local maintenance, booking, help and complaint responsibility;
- connect centres to transit and the heritage common;
- begin three controlled industry tests.

## Phase 3 — complete missing functions

Only after local evidence:

- reuse buildings for missing productive, learning, service or daily-life uses;
- expand shared research/enterprise facilities;
- improve neighbourhood green and public-space networks;
- add selective construction where reuse cannot meet a verified need;
- adopt longer-term affordability, access and operator agreements if lawful and feasible;
- strengthen inter-neighbourhood exchange without centralising all functions.

## Phase 4 — federation operations

- shared annual programme and international communication;
- recurring comparison of access, daily-life completeness and innovation outcomes;
- facility/service exchange among neighbourhoods;
- regional research and scenario partnerships;
- periodic decision to continue, adapt, decentralise or retire AI scenarios.

## Policy and operating directions

These are conceptual suggestions:

- negotiated public access to selected ground-floor and shared spaces;
- leases or operating agreements that preserve public hours and maintenance;
- small-team space and service access without exclusive vendor dependence;
- event limits and neighbourhood quiet commitments;
- public reporting of service hours, failures, complaints and withdrawal;
- anti-displacement monitoring before claims of “talent attraction.”

## Metrics to define after baseline

- percentage of users reaching a defined set of daily services by an accessible route;
- public-room hours that do not require purchase or registration;
- ordinary-day use across age and worker groups;
- number and duration of unresolved access gaps;
- local service and facility deficits by neighbourhood;
- shared-space availability and operator reliability;
- event days versus ordinary-service days;
- AI scenario continuation, modification and withdrawal decisions;
- resident/worker displacement indicators defined by qualified experts.

Targets are not invented in v0.1.

## Implementation risks

- boundaries and neighbourhood centres move after official data;
- operators cannot be identified;
- complete-neighbourhood language becomes a pretext for new development;
- service and public-space costs are ignored;
- local completeness duplicates facilities inefficiently;
- existing residents, merchants and institutions do not support proposed access;
- long-term affordability requires policy authority the concept does not possess.

## Correction protocol

When evidence contradicts a neighbourhood claim:

1. record the contradiction;
2. remove or relabel the unsupported claim;
3. update the affected spatial layer, programme and scenario;
4. compare reuse, relocation and deletion;
5. obtain owner/professional decision for material changes;
6. preserve the rejected reasoning.
