# Candidate 02 Three Key Areas v0.1

All locations and building actions are conceptual. The current key-area polygons are provisional, are not anchored reliably enough for parcel decisions, and must be replaced and reworked when authoritative geometry and existing-condition data become available.

## 1. Zhongzhiyuan AI Independent-Innovation Acceleration Area

### Positioning

**Making Neighbourhood / 造研邻区**

A complete productive neighbourhood where research, engineering, verification and repair coexist with food, rest, learning, worker services and an accessible public realm.

### Spatial structure

- a controlled productive edge for laboratories, engineering, loading and test functions;
- a public research centre for learning, review and enterprise/public interfaces;
- a five-minute worker-life web connecting transit, food, health, recreation and rest;
- a quiet ecological edge toward verified river/green relationships;
- clear separation and managed handoff between public, research and logistics routes.

### Functional layout

- R&D, engineering and flexible pilot spaces;
- shared instruments, compute/service advice and compliance support;
- training, technical education and public review rooms;
- ordinary food, convenience, exercise and health-support functions;
- short-stay/team-support concepts only after housing and ownership evidence;
- park/green interfaces that work without demonstrations.

### Building renewal

- identify reusable R&D, industrial and office fabric through a real inventory;
- adapt lower floors for shared support, review and learning;
- keep secure labs and logistics in controlled back-of-house zones;
- use reversible fit-out for changing teams;
- consider additions only where structure, fire, planning, environment and servicing are verified.

### Public space

The centre is a **Making Commons**, not a robot plaza: shaded seating, staff-supported information, public review, food and ordinary lunchtime use are permanent; demonstrations are temporary and bounded.

### Transport and slow mobility

- verify station/stop connections before drawing routes;
- continuous accessible walking between work, services and green space;
- cycling access and secure parking;
- separate technical loading from the civic centre;
- time-manage test movement and deliveries.

### AI scenarios

- shared-lab and compute-service navigator;
- controlled model/edge-system validation;
- equipment maintenance triage;
- accessible workday navigation;
- training and safety explanation;
- public review of selected, cleared outputs.

AI remains out of secure research and personal life unless explicitly authorised.

### Daily life

The neighbourhood must support:

- early and late work without making 24-hour labour the norm;
- affordable food and non-consumption rest;
- exercise and outdoor recovery;
- shift and service workers;
- visiting teams and trainees;
- people with disabilities.

### Main implementation risks

- a specialist campus remains closed and incomplete despite new branding;
- public space becomes a demonstration forecourt;
- housing or daily services are asserted without land/market evidence;
- logistics conflicts with walking and ecology;
- shared facilities lack a real operator and maintenance budget.

## 2. Beijing AI Origin Community

### Positioning

**Learning Neighbourhood / 共学邻区**

A complete university-city neighbourhood where research translation, entrepreneurship, intergenerational learning, care and ordinary community life reinforce one another.

### Spatial structure

- a civic learning centre at the overlap of public service, entrepreneurship and community life;
- a network of small work/learning rooms and third places;
- protected quiet residential, education and care interiors;
- active, legible edges toward transit, campuses and the heritage common;
- several local gardens and courtyards rather than one event plaza.

### Functional layout

- open learning, mentoring and peer-review space;
- small-team and early-stage enterprise support;
- legal, IP, recruitment and compute/data advice;
- community health, care, family and accessible service interfaces;
- food, retail, recreation and cultural uses;
- ordinary housing-support functions as a study topic, not a promised quantity.

### Building renewal

- audit ground floors, vacant periods and community facilities;
- open suitable lower floors through agreements rather than assumed access;
- reuse small buildings and rooms for learning and enterprise support;
- protect residential access, privacy, daylight and quiet;
- avoid replacing mixed fine-grain fabric with a single innovation complex.

### Public space

The centre is an **Origin Learning House** with indoor and outdoor public rooms, static information, human service and non-commercial places to stay. Its success is repeat use by residents and students, not event attendance.

### Transport and slow mobility

- prioritise walking and accessibility between local services, transit interfaces and public rooms;
- manage cycling parking without blocking footways;
- keep event arrival and delivery away from quiet interiors;
- verify all campus gates, crossings and station links before detailed design.

### AI scenarios

- public learning and translation assistant;
- entrepreneurship/service navigator;
- accessible-route information;
- community room scheduling;
- care-service information with human referral;
- public feedback summarisation with explicit consent and moderation.

### Daily life

The neighbourhood must work for:

- students and researchers;
- residents and families;
- older people and people with disabilities;
- service workers and small merchants;
- new teams and visitors;
- people who do not use an app or AI.

### Main implementation risks

- innovation branding accelerates displacement or service-price pressure;
- university access is assumed rather than negotiated;
- public rooms become membership spaces;
- evening activity harms residential life;
- “community participation” is simulated without residents.

## 3. Dazhongsi AI Industry Cluster

### Positioning

**Metropolitan Neighbourhood / 都会邻区**

A complete station-area city quarter where enterprise exchange, culture, commerce, arrival and ordinary neighbourhood services coexist beyond office hours.

### Spatial structure

- a legible transit-arrival and ground-level public-space sequence;
- a metropolitan civic centre combining enterprise service, culture and public help;
- mixed commercial, work, learning and community streets;
- service/logistics edges that protect walking and station operations;
- quiet links to nearby communities and heritage constraints after verification.

### Functional layout

- enterprise, capital, IP and international-service interfaces;
- flexible conference, exhibition and small launch spaces;
- repair, after-sales and intelligent-product service;
- culture, food, evening learning and ordinary retail;
- station help, accessible rest and non-digital information;
- community services that remain when events close.

### Building renewal

- adapt commercial and office lower floors for visible exchange and service;
- reuse underused halls or frontages where verified;
- improve entrances, canopies and ground-level continuity;
- introduce selective infill only after heritage, transit, structure, ownership and control review;
- avoid a sealed podium or iconic tower as the neighbourhood’s only identity.

### Public space

The centre is a **Metropolitan Exchange Hall** distributed across a station forecourt, public rooms and active streets. At least half of its ordinary functions should remain meaningful without an exhibition or AI activation; the exact performance measure requires later definition.

### Transport and slow mobility

- ground-level arrival and accessibility before bridge/tunnel speculation;
- clear walking and cycling approach from all verified station sides;
- managed taxi, pick-up/drop-off, event queues and deliveries;
- secure cycle parking and luggage-friendly routes;
- logistics separated from primary arrival space.

### AI scenarios

- multilingual arrival assistance;
- enterprise and event navigation;
- accessible transfer information;
- product-service and repair triage;
- cultural interpretation;
- event capacity support with staff control.

### Daily life

The quarter serves commuters, residents, students, visitors, enterprise teams, merchants, maintenance staff and evening users. Toilets, drinking water, seating, food, wayfinding and staffed help are basic urban infrastructure, not AI scenarios.

### Main implementation risks

- the current provisional Dazhongsi geometry is not reliably anchored to the named station area;
- the concept collapses into a generic TOD and exhibition centre;
- event and commercial pressure displace non-consuming public use;
- heritage and neighbourhood constraints are discovered late;
- ground-level access remains broken while attention shifts to landmark architecture.

## Shared completeness review

The three areas are not complete if the following remain external to them:

- essential daily services;
- accessible routes and staffed help;
- ordinary food and non-consumption rest;
- maintenance and logistics responsibility;
- quiet space and environmental comfort;
- public learning and transparent service information.

They are not differentiated if each receives the same “innovation hall + plaza + twelve AI nodes” formula.
