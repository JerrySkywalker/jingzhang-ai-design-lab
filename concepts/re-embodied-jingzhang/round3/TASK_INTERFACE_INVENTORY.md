This inventory demonstrates how public-purpose tasks can create, shrink or eliminate physical programmes. Values are synthetic test fixtures; real tasks require Owner observation, community process and professional checks.

## Public task admission packet

Every proposed spatial allocation must carry:

1. public purpose and affected user;
2. non-AI baseline and “do not automate” option;
3. observable state, maximum tolerable error and TTL;
4. prohibited data and privacy ceiling;
5. minimum sensing/compute/energy/tool bundle;
6. compatible platforms and excluded platforms;
7. resource shareability class and reset/isolation condition;
8. qualified human role, span-of-control cap and staff welfare need;
9. ordinary building/street space that may already suffice;
10. degraded action for cell, power, network, contamination and operator failure;
11. removal/reuse plan under 80% lower demand;
12. professional/official approvals and data still missing.

If the packet cannot answer items 1–4, the task does not enter infrastructure design. If ordinary service space satisfies items 5–10, no AI-specific facility is created.

## Interface consequences by layer

| Layer | Task-derived question | Possible answer | Spatial decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| public front | who explains, stops and substitutes the service? | ordinary staffed desk sufficient | use civic/operations counter; no branded station |
| controlled handoff | must a platform, payload or person cross a safety boundary? | low-risk supervised transfer | marked indoor threshold/holding point |
| service edge | does recovery require vehicle/platform access? | yes, time-bounded | conventional loading/service edge with queue cap |
| storage | can modules, human-contact and hazardous items share? | often no | separate locked rooms/lockers; may kill sharing |
| cleaning/waste | what reset closes a task? | general, hygiene or contaminated | separate wash/waste route or reject co-location |
| energy | is a common protected interface valid? | only for compatible low-voltage modules | removable connection in existing service space |
| compute/network | can local/offline operation meet safety? | task dependent | isolate rack or use no automation; public route never depends on it |
| recovery | what happens when platform or cell fails? | manual safe removal | isolated holding plus service route; not pedestrian space |

## Site use

The common contextual base can identify street, rail, named service and building-footprint leads. It cannot prove that a particular room, curb or route is lawful, available, accessible or correctly located. Therefore this inventory feeds typological sections and field/professional questions, not exact placement.
