# Building Interface Types

## Type hierarchy

| Type | Programme | Where it belongs | Permanent? | Admission rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 ordinary civic/operations room | staffed help, meeting, paper/phone service, staff welfare | public or building ground floor | yes as ordinary city service | always preferred before AI-specific space |
| B1 accountability front | operator/status/stop/appeal/explanation integrated with B0 | public-facing edge | removable fit-out | only with named qualified operator and non-digital baseline |
| B2 lightweight local service cell | module locker, supervised handoff, small safe holding, local/offline control | behind B0 or ordinary operations room | removable | only low-risk compatible tasks; no hazardous/human-contact mixing |
| B3 general distributed shared cell | general bay, low-voltage/compute interface, calibration/reset, independent shutdown | existing service wing/court | adaptable, not single-purpose | requires observed stagger plus a second independent cell/manual recovery |
| B4 specialised backend | hazardous/hygiene/technical repair, quarantine, waste route, certified energy/tool system | controlled service/industrial/research back-of-house | only if demand and professional evidence justify | not duplicated in all key areas; may remain dedicated |
| B5 temporary staging | event or short trial modules, capped holding | ordinary event/service space | temporary/mobile | leave no stranded slab or permanent queue after event |

## Front–middle–back section

### Public front

- ordinary entrance and accessible route;
- named human help and non-AI service;
- responsible operator, status, hours, stop/complaint/appeal;
- no compulsory app, registration or device encounter;
- shade/refuge and ordinary use when the technical layer is closed.

### Controlled middle

- supervised handoff and queue cap;
- safe stop/holding without blocking egress;
- lockable boundary between public and technical domains;
- incident closure record and offline controls;
- no repair noise, hazardous work or battery queue.

### Back-of-house

- ordinary service access and manual recovery;
- general bay and separate specialist spaces as required;
- clean, human-contact and hazardous stores/wash/waste routes separated;
- energy/compute rooms subject to fire, power, ventilation and network review;
- operator work/welfare and line-of-sight appropriate to responsibility;
- independent cell shutdown domains.

## Retain/adapt/build decision

1. reuse an ordinary operations/civic room if it satisfies the task;
2. fit removable lockers/status/handoff components;
3. adapt an existing service bay with isolation;
4. add a small reversible service wing only after measured demand;
5. consider stand-alone specialised backend only after building/fire/energy/logistics survey proves ordinary space inadequate.

No OSM footprint is treated as evidence of use, vacancy, ownership, structure or availability.

## AI-off and low-demand conversion

| Type | AI off | 80% lower demand |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | unchanged | unchanged |
| B1 | static operator/service notice | fold into B0 |
| B2 | ordinary storage/help/operations | remove modules; retain room |
| B3 | municipal/building maintenance, cycle/mobility-aid repair or general service bay | use one bay; mothball/remove fit-out from the other |
| B4 | conventional specialist workshop only if an independent ordinary need exists | do not build unless that need is demonstrated |
| B5 | remove | remove |

## Building-survey kill questions

- Can independent fire/power/shutdown domains actually fit?
- Are service access and manual recovery lawful and safe?
- Can storage/wash/waste separation be achieved without consuming public/green space?
- Does staff welfare/oversight fit without displacing ordinary service?
- Is an existing room genuinely public/accessible rather than a private showroom?
- Will noise, heat rejection, energy or night logistics harm adjacent living/learning space?

A “no” to any critical question removes or relocates the technical programme; it does not justify more automation.
