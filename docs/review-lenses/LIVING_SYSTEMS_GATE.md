# Living Systems Gate

## Purpose and status

This is a review lens extracted from Candidate 03 after its standalone status was killed by ADR-0002. It is not a candidate, a style guide, an ecological certification or a claim about existing site performance. It can audit any urban-design proposal, including Candidates 01 and 02.

The gate asks whether a scheme first makes a physically liveable, recoverable city and then adds technology. It prevents AI from compensating for missing shade, inaccessible routes, poor drainage, inadequate refuge or unmaintainable public space.

## Evidence discipline

Every row must receive exactly one status:

```text
PASS
CONCERN
BLOCKED_BY_EVIDENCE
REQUIRES_SPATIAL_CHANGE
```

`PASS` requires a specific spatial or operating response and evidence appropriate to the current design stage. `BLOCKED_BY_EVIDENCE` is not a pass and must name the smallest missing observation, survey or official source. A concept image, AI forecast or generic benchmark cannot establish site performance. No numeric ecological or comfort target may be invented.

## Gate questions

| Review subject | Falsification questions | Minimum inspectable evidence | Escalation signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing living assets | What existing trees, planted areas, spontaneous vegetation, habitat edges and everyday uses would be retained? Has absence of evidence been mistaken for absence of value? | Dated public source, bounded field observation or professional survey; explicit unknowns | Existing assets are erased by a blank-slate diagram or technology footprint |
| Soil | Where can soil remain continuous, uncompacted and serviceable? What excavation, slab, duct, hub or event load would seal or fragment it? | Concept footprint and servicing logic now; soil/arboricultural survey later | Permanent equipment occupies the only plausible rooting or infiltration space |
| Water and drainage | Where does rain go in normal and intense events? Can paths, refuges and service areas fail safely without shifting risk elsewhere? | Catchment hypothesis marked provisional; official drainage/flood data and specialist test before engineering claims | Scheme needs active sensing to conceal a missing physical drainage route |
| Canopy and shade | Are walking, waiting, working and refuge spaces protected in heat without relying on screens or cooling gadgets? Are tree growth and maintenance space preserved? | Sun/shade logic and existing-canopy evidence; landscape review | Signature plazas or test areas displace shade or cannot support mature canopy |
| Seasonal comfort | Does the ordinary route still work in heat, rain, winter wind/snow and poor-air conditions? Are alternatives close, legible and publicly accessible? | Scenario-by-season review; later microclimate and accessibility assessment | Only the render-day weather state works |
| Habitat continuity | Does the proposal preserve or strengthen connected habitat rather than scatter decorative patches? How are disturbance, lighting and operating hours limited? | Continuity diagram plus evidence caveats; ecological survey before claims | Linear infrastructure, night activity or logistics create an unacknowledged barrier |
| Public refuge | Can a person stop, sit, cool down, shelter, breathe, recover attention, obtain water or seek help without purchase, registration or AI? | Route-and-refuge sequence, non-digital access and operating responsibility | Refuge is commercial, event-only, locked, app-gated or too far from daily routes |
| Maintenance | Who can reach soil, planting, drainage, equipment and public facilities, with what physical access and shutdown mode? Does maintenance displace pedestrians or landscape? | Maintenance path, access zone, storage and responsibility hypothesis | A clean render hides permanent service occupation or unstaffed care work |
| Recovery | After heat, flood, equipment failure, event overload or landscape damage, what remains usable and how is the public informed? | Degraded-state spatial diagram and recovery sequence | Failure turns the public realm into a fenced technical site |
| Public accessibility | Are the best blue-green spaces usable without invitation, payment, biometric identity, a smartphone or participation in a test? | Entry conditions and non-digital route | Public value depends on access to a private campus, platform or programme |
| Logistics and event pressure | Where do deliveries, waste, charging, repair, queues, temporary stages and emergency access go? What do they do to soil, routes and refuge? | Ordinary-day/event-day overlays and safe recovery routes | Peak events consume the everyday public realm or block maintenance |
| Technology footprint | Which physical problem is each AI element solving? Would shade, drainage, legibility, staffing or a simpler spatial repair solve it better? | AI-on/AI-off comparison including footprint and maintenance | AI is used to compensate for a preventable physical-design failure |

## Required review output

For each proposal, publish a table containing:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| `STATUS` | One of the four allowed values |
| `FINDING` | The precise pass, concern, evidence gap or required spatial change |
| `EVIDENCE` | File/source/observation, or `none available` |
| `PHYSICAL_CONSEQUENCE` | What land, building, street, public-space, landscape or operating arrangement changes |
| `NEXT_GATE` | Smallest professional, field or official-data action needed |

The review ends with two short lists:

1. `NON_NEGOTIABLE_SPATIAL_CHANGES` — changes required before the candidate advances;
2. `EVIDENCE_GAPS_NOT_DESIGN_LICENSES` — unknowns that must remain unknown rather than be filled with attractive assumptions.

## Anti-convergence rule

Applying this gate must not turn every proposal into Candidate 03. It constrains failure, ecological loss and public exclusion; it does not prescribe a candidate's urban structure, industrial model, brand or primary thesis.
